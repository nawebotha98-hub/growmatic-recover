# Market validation — is the opportunity real?

Researched 28 July 2026, in response to Ewan asking for real evidence the
Quote Recovery opportunity exists, not just internal reasoning. This
supplements `STRATEGY.md`'s original competitor research (BizAI) with what
a fresh round of research turned up — including some competitors the
original scoring didn't have in view. Read this alongside `STRATEGY.md`;
it doesn't replace that document's decision, but it does sharpen it.

## Verdict, upfront

**The pain point is real and well-documented. The market is real and large
enough. The competitive space is more crowded than the original strategy
memo accounted for — not empty, but not closed either.** The product needs
a sharper, narrower pitch than "we track your quotes" to win against
competitors who already do that. Details and the specific recommendation
are below.

## 1. Is "quotes go cold and nobody follows up" a real, provable pain point?

Yes, strongly, both generically and in South Africa specifically:

- 48% of salespeople never make a single follow-up attempt after initial
  contact. 80% of B2B deals need 5+ follow-ups to close, but 92% of reps
  quit after 4. A few extra follow-up attempts can lift conversion by up to
  70%. ([DreamMaker](https://dreammakerr.com/follow-up-statistics/), [Martal](https://martal.ca/sales-follow-up-statistics-lb/))
- This isn't just a global stat — multiple companies exist *only* to solve
  this: [QuoteTrack](https://quotetrack-landing.vercel.app/) (quote pipeline
  visibility, flags stale/at-risk quotes), [Quote Revival](https://quoterevival.com/)
  (re-engaging old quotes), and Level (pulls quote-aging reports from field
  service software, flags a literal "7-day rule" before a quote goes cold).
  Nobody builds a company around a fake problem — this confirms the pain is
  real enough to sustain businesses elsewhere.
- South Africa specifically: Hellopeter reviews document solar customers
  chasing installers for callbacks that never came ([example](https://www.hellopeter.com/solar-advice/reviews/no-service-after-multiple-attempts-to-get-a-quote-3701184)).
  Standard Bank data shows 74% of township businesses run on WhatsApp for
  trade — meaning the "quote sent as a WhatsApp message and then lost in
  the thread" scenario GrowMatic's pitch describes is not a hypothetical,
  it's how most small SA businesses actually operate.

## 2. Is the market big enough?

Per-vertical estimates found:

| Vertical | Estimated size |
|---|---|
| Solar installers | 400–600 active businesses nationally, ~150–200 SAPVIA members |
| Security (CCTV/access control/gate automation) | 16,000 PSIRA-registered companies overall (guarding services included — installation-specific subset smaller but still large); SAEFIA is the relevant electric-fence/gate-automation body |
| Commercial HVAC/refrigeration | Market valued at ~$1.86–1.99bn (2025/26), growing 6.6–7.8% CAGR — company count wasn't findable directly, but market value this size implies a substantial number of installer businesses |

Even conservatively, the four verticals combined plausibly represent several
thousand South African businesses matching the ICP (5–50 staff, real quote
volume). Starting in Gqeberha/Eastern Cape is a beachhead, not the ceiling.

## 3. Competition — the part that needs updating from the original strategy

`STRATEGY.md` scored this against one named competitor (BizAI, R599/mo, "AI
CRM + WhatsApp + Sage") and concluded the right move was to never compete
as a CRM — sit alongside existing tools instead. That reasoning holds, but
this research round found **closer, more specific competitors** the
original scoring didn't have:

- **ClientPulse** (clientpulse.co.za) — a WhatsApp-first CRM built
  specifically for South African small businesses. R1,100/month. Already
  does: quote + invoice sending with open/delivery tracking, pipeline
  tracking, automated follow-ups, POPIA-ready, PayFast billing. This is
  **closer to GrowMatic's pitch than BizAI was, and cheaper while doing
  more** — a real problem for the current R1,800/month price if the
  pitch isn't sharply differentiated.
- **Tradesman Buddy** (tradesmanbuddy.co.za) — R79/month, SA-specific,
  quotes/invoices/WhatsApp for trades (painters, tilers, electricians,
  etc.). Cheaper and more basic — less direct overlap, but still evidence
  this exact buyer is already being sold to.
- **ServiceM8, Jobber, Tradify** — established international field-service
  platforms, all of which now ship *automated multi-step quote follow-up*
  as a built-in or add-on feature. ServiceM8 confirmed operating in South
  Africa with ZAR pricing. These are the platforms a growing installer
  might already be using for scheduling/invoicing — meaning some prospects
  may already have a version of this feature and not know it, or have it
  switched off.

**What this means:** the "quote follow-up" feature itself is not a moat —
several competitors, local and international, already offer some version
of it, some cheaper than GrowMatic. The moat has to be the specific angle
`STRATEGY.md` already identified (imported *history* + compounding data +
zero switching cost) — but that angle now needs to be argued explicitly
against ClientPulse, not just BizAI, because ClientPulse's "sits alongside,
doesn't replace anything" pitch is nearly identical framing to GrowMatic's.

## 4. The actual differentiation that still holds up

The one thing none of the above competitors do: **a retrospective audit of
quotes already sent**, before asking for any commitment. ClientPulse,
Tradesman Buddy, ServiceM8/Jobber/Tradify are all *forward-looking* tools —
you adopt them and use them for new quotes going forward. None of them say
"send us your last 3 months of quotes and we'll show you, for free, the
rand value sitting there right now, before you sign up for anything."

That's the actual wedge: it's a diagnostic, not a CRM signup. It requires
zero tool switching (the entry ask is "export what you already have," not
"start using a new system"), the value is shown before the sale, and it's
one specific number a business owner cannot get from ClientPulse or
ServiceM8 without already being a paying, onboarded user of those tools.
This matches the "Founder operating rule" in `STRATEGY.md` (manual audit
first) — that instinct was correct, and this research reinforces *why*: the
free audit is not just a sales tactic, it's the actual product
differentiation against every competitor found above.

## 5. Recommendation

**Keep going — do not pivot the product.** But two adjustments this
research points to directly:

1. **Never let the pitch become "software for tracking quotes."** The
   moment a prospect mentally files GrowMatic next to ClientPulse or
   ServiceM8, it's a harder, more expensive sale it doesn't need to be. The
   pitch is always "let me show you the number in your own last 3 months of
   quotes" — a diagnostic, not a subscription pitch — exactly as
   `SALES-PLAYBOOK.md` already scripts it.
2. **Watch the R1,800/month price against ClientPulse's R1,100/month**
   during real conversations. If prospects push back on price by comparing
   to ClientPulse specifically (not hypothetically), that's a real signal
   to act on — either sharpen the "audit vs. CRM" distinction harder in the
   pitch, or reconsider the Founding Pilot price. Don't preemptively
   discount before that objection actually happens.

## Addendum, 28 July 2026 — a stronger angle surfaced mid-conversation

Ewan disclosed he has zero personal network in solar/security/gate/HVAC
(the current ICP), but has 10+ years of his own experience as a sales
executive in capital equipment. **Note: this is about Ewan's own
transferable background and skills, not his current employer — his 9-to-5
job is unrelated to GrowMatic SA and is explicitly out of scope for this
project; nothing here should reference it.** An ICP Ewan has genuine
industry experience and credibility in beats a cold vertical every time,
even if the cold vertical was validated on paper.

### Research: is "lost quotes" a real pain point in capital equipment sales?

Yes — arguably **more sharply evidenced** than the original trades ICP:

- **71% of capital equipment deals lost to competition involved a
  follow-up gap of 14+ days during the evaluation phase**
  ([source](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/AI-sales/why-sales-reps-stop-following-up-how-to-fix-it)).
  Buyers don't pause evaluating just because a rep goes quiet — they move
  to whichever vendor stays visible.
- **CRM abandonment in equipment dealerships is a documented, specific
  failure pattern**: dealerships that adopt standalone CRMs often see
  sales teams stop using them within 60 days, because the CRM doesn't
  know what's in stock, can't create a work order, can't pull current
  pricing — so reps fall back to personal spreadsheets and email threads
  ([Tractor Zoom Pro](https://www.tractorzoompro.com/blog/more-than-a-crm-sales-enablement-for-dealerships)).
  This is a stronger validation of the "sits alongside, doesn't replace"
  positioning than anything found for the trades ICP — it's not a guess
  that a lightweight tool would be preferred, it's documented that heavy
  tools get actively abandoned in this exact world.
- Existing competitors in this space (Flyntlok, Texada, Softbase, Annata,
  crmSeries) are full **Dealer Management Systems** — built for large
  dealers with parts inventory, rental fleets, and service departments
  (think heavy construction/agricultural equipment). None of them appear
  built for a smaller multi-product equipment seller (11-50 staff, no
  rental/service arm) — this looks like real whitespace, not because the
  pain is different, but because the existing tools are the wrong shape
  and size for this segment.
- SA-wide: only ~35% of SA small businesses use any CRM at all
  ([CRM Africa](https://crm.africa/blog/free-crm-for-small-business-south-africa-2/)) —
  general confirmation most SA SMEs, including equipment sellers, are
  still running on spreadsheets/email.
- Relevant SA industry bodies for outreach if this direction is taken:
  MTMA (Machine Tools Merchants' Association), SAAMA (agricultural
  machinery), SACEEC (capital equipment export), MEMSA (mining equipment).

### What's NOT yet verified

All of the above is evidence from adjacent contexts (heavy/construction/
agricultural equipment dealers), not proof that any specific smaller SA
multi-product machine seller has this exact problem day to day. It also
doesn't yet answer whether Ewan has real reachable contacts in this
industry *outside* his employer — that's the open question, not
something to resolve by using his current job's colleagues or customers,
which is explicitly off-limits for this project.

### Updated recommendation

This angle is only worth pursuing if Ewan has real industry access that
has nothing to do with his current employer (a previous role, industry
contacts made independently, associations, LinkedIn network built on his
own). If that access exists, this looks like a stronger opportunity than
the original solar/security/gate/HVAC ICP — same product, same codebase,
a market with sharper documented pain, and weaker/wrong-shaped existing
competition. If it doesn't exist independent of his job, this angle
should be dropped, since the entire rationale was real access — without
it, this is just another cold vertical, no better than the original one.
Not yet decided — pending Ewan's answer, not an executed pivot.
